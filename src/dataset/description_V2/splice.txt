This dataset is designed for predicting human splice donor and acceptor sites—critical genomic regions determining alternative splicing, which underpins protein diversity and relates to genetic disorders. Derived from the Ensembl GRCh38 genome (Wang et al., 2019), the original sequences are 400 bp long, containing 10,000 labeled examples: True (canonical GT-AG splice sites, i.e., donor sites at intron-start "GT" and acceptor sites at intron-end "AG") and False (non-splice-site regions). While existing models achieve near-perfect performance on this benchmark (Ji et al., 2021), they struggle with real-world generalization due to oversimplified data: non-canonical splice variants (e.g., GC-AG) are absent, and models fail to distinguish adversarial edge cases. To address this, we reconstruct the dataset using an iterative adversarial training strategy. This involves dynamically injecting unseen false positive predictions (from a hold-out validation set) into the training pool, forcing models to refine decision boundaries against challenging mimics of non-canonical sites or noisy genomic contexts. The enhanced dataset aims to improve robustness in detecting splice sites under realistic biological complexity, supporting applications in clinical variant interpretation (e.g., rare disease diagnostics) and exploration of non-canonical splicing mechanisms.
