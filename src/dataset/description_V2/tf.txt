Task: Binary classification to predicting human transcription factor (TF) binding sites—short genomic regions. Labels indicate whether the DNA sequence is bound by TFs (Ture) or not (False). TFs interact with DNA to regulate gene expression, critical for understanding transcriptional networks and therapeutic target discovery. Constructed from 690 legacy ENCODE ChIP-seq experiments (Consortium et al., 2012) sourced via the UCSC Genome Browser, it spans 161 distinct transcription factors profiled across 91 human cell lines, capturing diverse cellular and regulatory contexts. Each TF binding site (TFBS) is represented by a 101-bp sequence centered on ChIP-seq peak summits (True class), while the False class comprises non-overlapping, length- and GC content-matched background sequences to minimize compositional bias. To ensure the benchmark reflects realistic challenges, we curated a refined subset by filtering tasks at extremes of model performance: trivial cases (e.g., >0.95 F1 score, indicating oversimplified patterns) and overly complex cases (e.g., <0.50 F1 score, possibly due to noisy labels or data scarcity). From this filtered pool, 5 representative TF tasks were selected, balancing biological relevance, sequence diversity, and model-generalization potential. The final dataset enables robust evaluation of sequence-based TFBS prediction models, with applications in functional genomics (e.g., annotating regulatory variants) and drug development (e.g., identifying dysregulated TF targets in diseases like cancer).
