This dataset facilitates the prediction of SARS-CoV-2 viral variants using 1,000-base-pair (bp) genome sequence segments, critical for tracking viral evolution and guiding public health responses. Derived from the GISAID EpiCoV database (Khare et al., 2021), it encompasses nine globally significant variants designated by the World Health Organization (WHO), including Alpha (B.1.1.7), Beta (B.1.351), Delta (B.1.617.2), and others (Eta, Gamma, Iota, Kappa, Lambda, Zeta). Sequences are carefully curated to represent distinct lineages, ensuring genetic diversity while maintaining focus on epidemiologically relevant strains. Each sample captures key mutations (e.g., spike protein S-G614D in Delta) within the 1,000-bp windows, balancing sequence length for computational efficiency and resolution of variant-defining features. The dataset addresses challenges posed by overlapping mutations and convergent evolution by prioritizing high-quality, annotated genomes with minimal sequencing artifacts. By enabling variant classification at scale, it supports applications in outbreak surveillance (e.g., detecting emerging sublineages), vaccine efficacy studies (e.g., mapping antigenic drift), and genomic epidemiology (e.g., tracing transmission networks).
